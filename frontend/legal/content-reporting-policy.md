## Making a useful report

Include the type of concern, a brief explanation, a way to contact you if safe, and enough information to locate the content. A task or attachment identifier, account reference, or existing location may help. Do not obtain unauthorized access to gather evidence. Do not send passwords, refresh tokens, a complete private task archive, or unnecessary personal information.

Do not download, reproduce, or attach suspected child sexual abuse material or other illegal files just to report them. Provide identifiers and context; you do not need to obtain a private link or enter another person's workspace to report a concern. If there is an immediate threat to life or safety, contact the appropriate emergency services. Reports are handled manually by the operator. Our email inbox is not monitored as an emergency service and has no guaranteed round-the-clock response time. These support limitations do not extend any applicable legal deadline.

## How reports are handled

We assess reports using available information and applicable law, may ask for relevant missing information, and may restrict content or accounts when justified. We may need to preserve records or report to authorities. We provide a decision or explanation where appropriate and permitted, while protecting privacy, safety, and investigations. Do not assume that a report will be kept completely confidential: disclosure may be necessary for a legal process or a fair response. We avoid disclosing unnecessary personal information.

If your content or account is restricted and you believe this was a mistake, reply to the decision notice or email the contact address with the reference and your reasons. We will consider corrections and review the restriction where legally permitted. Any statutory notice, counter-notice, appeal, or court procedure remains available where applicable.

Other applicable legal duties remain in force. Publication of this policy does not certify that the necessary operational response process is already established.

## Copyright notice requirements

If you own copyright or are authorized to act for the owner, identify the protected work and the allegedly infringing content with enough detail to locate it. For a notice intended to comply with US DMCA requirements, include:

1. Your physical or electronic signature.
2. Identification of the copyrighted work, or a representative list where appropriate.
3. Identification and location of the material you seek to have removed or disabled.
4. Your name and contact information, including address, telephone number, and email address.
5. A statement that you have a good-faith belief the disputed use is not authorized by the copyright owner, its agent, or law.
6. A statement that the notice is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that you are authorized to act for the owner of the exclusive right allegedly infringed.

Consider authorization and legally permitted uses, including fair use, before making a claim. Knowingly material misrepresentations may expose the sender to liability. We may forward the relevant notice to the affected user as permitted or required.

## Counter-notices and repeat infringement

Where the DMCA process applies and material has been removed for copyright reasons, a qualifying counter-notice generally needs your signature, identification and former location of the removed material, a statement under penalty of perjury that removal resulted from mistake or misidentification, and your name, address, and telephone number. It must also include consent to the relevant US federal district court's jurisdiction and acceptance of service from the claimant or its agent; for a person outside the United States, the statute specifies jurisdiction where the provider may be found.

Under the applicable DMCA process, a qualifying counter-notice is forwarded to the claimant. Restoration ordinarily occurs no earlier than 10 and no later than 14 business days after receipt unless the designated agent receives notice of a filed court action seeking to restrain the alleged infringement. Restoration is not promised where separate legal prohibitions or valid restrictions apply. Seek advice before submitting a counter-notice because it includes legal declarations and may lead to litigation.

Donee's policy is to terminate repeat infringers in appropriate circumstances. This requires actual implementation, not merely publication of these words. Applicable law controls if a deadline or procedure differs from this summary.
